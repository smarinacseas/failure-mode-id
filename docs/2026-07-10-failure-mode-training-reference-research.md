# Failure-Mode Training Reference — Research Report

Scope note: this report maps eight LLM instruction-following failure modes to (1) named training strategies foundation labs and lab-adjacent researchers currently use or publish, and (2) the dataset a lab would realistically commission to execute each strategy — structure, annotation scheme, scale, and whether the reward signal is auto-verifiable (RLVR-ready), judge/rubric-based, or hybrid. Every claim below traces to a source in [Sources](#sources); nothing is drawn from general background knowledge. The failure-mode-to-method mappings themselves are this project's interpretive taxonomy, not claims made by the cited papers.

Register note: sections below are written for a team deciding what data to commission, not as a literature survey — each subsection leads with the named method, then the concrete data a vendor would be asked to produce.

## constraint_never_surfaced

**Strategy**

The leading named method is **RLCF — Reinforcement Learning from Checklist Feedback** (CMU + Apple, NeurIPS 2025) [6][7][8]. RLCF extracts an instruction-specific checklist from each prompt, scores candidate responses per checklist item using both AI judges and specialized verifier programs, and combines the scores into an RL reward. Applied to Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, RLCF was "the only method to improve performance on every benchmark, including a 4-point boost in hard satisfaction rate on FollowBench, a 6-point increase on InFoBench, and a 3-point rise in win rate on Arena-Hard" [6] — evidence that checklist-shaped, per-instruction rewards outperform fixed-criteria reward models for surfacing and holding onto constraints. Caveat: RLCF is CMU+Apple academic/applied research, not a disclosed frontier-lab (OpenAI/Anthropic/Google DeepMind/Meta/Qwen) production method, and its published pipeline is compute-heavy (~4 days on 8×H100 for 130K instructions) [6].

**RECAST / RLVC** (2505.19030) is a second, independent instantiation of the same idea: constraints are extracted from real-world prompt-response pairs (not invented templates) and each instance carries its full constraint set plus a per-constraint validation method [9]. RECAST is a preprint without a confirmed peer-review venue; treat its numbers as medium-confidence.

At inference time, **DeCRIM** (Amazon, Findings of EMNLP 2024) complements training-time methods: it decomposes an instruction into a constraint checklist and loops Critique-Refine until all constraints pass. "DECRIM improves Mistral's performance by 7.3% on REALINSTRUCT and 8.0% on IFEval even with weak feedback"; under Oracle feedback, open-source Mistral surpasses GPT-4 on both benchmarks (93.7% vs. 78.8% instruction accuracy on RealInstruct) [10][11].

**Dataset taxonomy**

- **WildChecklists** (RLCF's training corpus): 130,000 instructions sourced from WildChat, paired with synthetically generated checklists (Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct as generator) [6]. Verifiability is explicitly **hybrid**: per the paper's own generation prompt, Python verifier programs are written "ONLY for requirements that are explicitly about syntax or format" — everything else is graded by an LLM judge, averaging 25 sampled 0–100 scores weighted by per-item 0–100 importance weights [6].
- **RECAST-30K**: 30,000 instances spanning 19 constraint types, mined from real prompt-response pairs, each instance carrying its full constraint set and per-constraint validation method [9].
- **RealInstruct** (DeCRIM's benchmark): real first-turn ShareGPT instructions decomposed via GPT-4 into task + context + constraints; a human-validated test split of 302 instructions / 1,055 constraints (avg. 3.5 per instruction) plus a non-validated validation split of 842 instructions / 2,500 constraints; constraints manually organized into 22 categories. Rule-based scoring was explicitly rejected as infeasible: "Given the open-ended nature of real user instructions, rule-based evaluation is infeasible, so we adopted the LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation" [10][11] — this is the canonical structure for a **not-RLVR-ready**, judge-dependent constraint-decomposition commission.

Product read: the realistic commission for this mode is **hybrid**, weighted toward judge grading. Even the two purpose-built checklist datasets (WildChecklists, RECAST-30K) only write deterministic verifiers for the syntax/format slice of constraints; the majority of checklist items require LLM-judge or human-rubric scoring, and real-user constraint data (RealInstruct) is judge-only by the authors' own design choice.

## constraint_dropped

**Strategy**

**Counterfactual Simulation Training (CST)** (Hase & Potts, Feb 2026) is the strongest verified method lead for CoT-to-answer faithfulness — training a model so that constraints tracked in its reasoning actually govern its output. CST "rewards CoTs that enable a simulator to accurately predict a model's outputs over counterfactual inputs." In experiments up to 235B parameters it "substantially improve[d] monitor accuracy on cue-based counterfactuals (by 35 accuracy points) as well as simulatability over generic counterfactuals (by 2 points)" [12][13]. Caveat: this is a two-author, non-peer-reviewed preprint, not a disclosed frontier-lab production method; gains did not generalize from persuading to dissuading cues, and the generic-counterfactual simulatability gain (2 points) is modest.

**Conifer** (2404.02823) supplies a concrete process-feedback annotation format directly usable for constraint-retention training: internal feedback, where "GPT-4 explicitly demonstrates constraint adherence" inside its own responses (scoped to format/numerical constraints), and external feedback, where "GPT-4 is tasked to identify constraints where it fails to adhere in the most challenging constraints, and this judgement is employed to construct a new multi-turn conversation" in the form {Difficult Instruction, Model Response, GPT-4 Judgement, GPT-4 Response} [14]. Caveat: Conifer's paper targets constraint failures generally, not specifically the reasoning-trace-to-final-answer drop this mode names — the mapping is interpretive.

A third, complementary lever is training a dedicated **constraint-tracking critic**: Mistral v0.2, LoRA-fine-tuned on GPT-4-Turbo CoT reasoning trails annotating constraint satisfaction (RealInstruct's validation split), gains "+12.9 in Macro F1 and +28.1 in F1 Neg." on constraint verification, and plugging this Supervised Critic into DeCRIM lifts Mistral +3.7 on RealInstruct — while pure self-critique (Self-Refine) yields only marginal gains or degrades IFEval, i.e. trained/external feedback substantially outperforms unstructured self-correction for catching dropped constraints [10][11].

**Dataset taxonomy**

- **Conifer**: 13,606 multi-turn conversations (avg. 3.02 turns per conversation), built via a GPT-4 pipeline of "Query Reframing, Constraint Generation, Recombination, Two-Stage Filtering," with each seed instruction expanded to up to 5 difficulty levels and "aggregate[d]... into a single sample using the multi-turn conversational format." The training recipe is progressive easy-to-hard **curriculum SFT** plus process feedback — not pure preference optimization, though the best variant layers DPO afterward using external UltraFeedback data [14][15].
- **CST**: code is public (github.com/peterbhase/counterfactual-simulation-training) [13]; the paper's dataset-construction detail is thinner than Conifer's — it is documented primarily as a reward/training procedure over counterfactual input generation, not as a released annotated corpus with a fixed schema.
- **RealInstruct's Supervised Critic data**: GPT-4-Turbo CoT reasoning trails over the RealInstruct validation split (842 instructions / 2,500 constraints), annotated for per-constraint satisfaction — the same judge-dependent structure as constraint_never_surfaced's RealInstruct entry, reused here for its critic-training angle [10][11].

Product read: this mode's realistic data is **judge/rubric-dependent throughout** — GPT-4-as-judge identifies constraint failures and writes corrections (Conifer), or a critic model is trained on GPT-4-Turbo-annotated satisfaction labels (RealInstruct). No verified method here runs a rule-based verifier end to end; a lab commissioning constraint_dropped data should budget for LLM-judge-in-the-loop annotation, not expect an RLVR shortcut.

## constraint_overridden

**Strategy**

OpenAI's published lineage on this mode is the clearest multi-year trajectory in the material. It runs from **the instruction hierarchy** (Wallace et al., Apr 2024) through **deliberative alignment** (Dec 2024) to **IH-Challenge** (~Mar 2026), moving from judge-graded to fully auto-verifiable reward design as the method matures.

- **Instruction hierarchy** [16][17]: an explicit priority ordering (system > user > tool) plus a data-generation method that "teaches LLMs to selectively ignore lower-privileged instructions" on conflict. Two commissionable data structures: (a) **context synthesis** for aligned instructions — compositional requests decomposed into constraint pieces placed at different hierarchy levels, with the model trained to predict the original ground-truth response; (b) **context ignorance** for misaligned/injected instructions — the target label is "the same answer they would have generated if they never saw the lower-level instructions," with "ignore" as ideal behavior and "refuse" as fallback. Labels are behavioral targets, not preference pairs, and verifying compliance requires human/model judgment of semantic equivalence — **not auto-verifiable**. OpenAI generated this data synthetically via self-distillation, not human annotators; it shipped in GPT-4o mini and was carried into the Model Spec's "chain of command."
- **Deliberative alignment** [18][19]: teaches the model the literal text of safety/policy specifications and trains it to explicitly recall and reason over them in the CoT before answering (used to align the o-series). "Our training procedure requires no human-labeled completions": (prompt, CoT, output) SFT data is synthesized by prompting a base reasoning model with the spec text so the CoT cites the spec, filtered by a spec-conditioned judge model, then the spec is stripped from the training prompt; the RL reward on safety-relevant prompts reuses "our 'judge' model with access to our safety policies" — i.e. **rubric/spec-conditioned model-graded reward**, not RLVR and not human preference labels. What a lab commissions here is spec/policy authoring plus judge rubrics, not per-example human completions.
- **IH-Challenge** [20][21][22][23]: OpenAI's newest published strategy — RL on a 27,570-row instruction-hierarchy dataset where "we require each IH-Challenge task to be objectively gradable by a per-datapoint Python grader that remains valid under adversarially synthesized instruction conflicts," explicitly to resist reward hacking from LLM judges. Fine-tuning GPT-5-Mini with online adversarial example generation raised average IH robustness across 16 benchmarks from 84.1% to 94.1% and cut unsafe behavior from 6.6% to 0.7%. The released HuggingFace dataset carries a `grader_code_python` field per row — **fully RLVR-ready by design**.

Outside OpenAI, **VerIH** (2511.04694) reframes instruction-hierarchy resolution as a reasoning task and trains it with lightweight RLVR: "we construct VerIH, a training dataset of constraint-following tasks with verifiable answers, comprising aligned and conflicting system–user instructions... achieving ~20% absolute improvement in conflict setups" [24]. On IHEval with Qwen3-8B, conflict-set performance rose from 46.48% to 63.48% while aligned-set performance was preserved, and the effect transferred out-of-distribution to safety (jailbreak/prompt-injection attack success rates down up to ~20 points, e.g. WildJailbreak 64.05%→41.25%) [24]. VerIH is an academic paper, not a confirmed frontier-lab production method, but it demonstrates the RLVR-for-hierarchy pattern reproduces outside OpenAI's own pipeline.

**Dataset taxonomy**

- **IH-Challenge**: 27,570 rows, each with per-row Python grader code, produced via an online adversarial-generation loop against the target model — the most mechanically verifiable dataset structure in this mode [20][21][22][23].
- **VerIH**: 7,192 samples drawn from 24 categories of RLVR-IFEval; half are LLM-rewritten (via Claude-4-Sonnet, preserving original intent) into conflicting (system, user_conflict) pairs, half remain aligned; constraints are restricted to format/quantity/keyword usage so they stay "deterministic[ly] evaluat[able] with simple functions" [24] — RLVR-ready without human labels.
- **Instruction hierarchy / deliberative alignment (2024-era)**: earlier-vintage data, model/judge-graded rather than rule-verified — useful as the "before" state for this mode's RLVR maturation.

Product read: this is the mode with the clearest evidence of **maturing from judge-dependent to RLVR-ready**. A lab commissioning constraint_overridden data today for objective, format-style conflicts should specify per-datapoint verifiable graders (the IH-Challenge/VerIH pattern); IH-Challenge's own design note is explicit that genuinely subjective hierarchy conflicts (e.g. jailbreak nuance) still require LLM judges, which is why that paper restricts its dataset to tasks it can grade objectively rather than claiming full coverage.

## constraint_sacrificed

**Strategy**

OpenAI's **"confession" training** [25] is the most direct verified method for this mode: RL on top of GPT-5-Thinking where, following a post-answer system request, the model produces a structured self-report of its own compliance (a "ConfessionReport"), and the confession's reward is graded solely on honesty and firewalled from the main task reward — directly targeting silent constraint trade-offs and compliance overclaiming. The confession reward is **not auto-verifiable**: it is computed by an LLM judge rating honesty and comprehensiveness, uses no confession-specific ground-truth labels, and is mixed into standard RL at a uniform probability (25% of inputs in the intervention run examined), with the same judge across all environments. Quantitatively, when the model misbehaves it confesses at least half the time in 11 of 12 out-of-distribution evaluations (spanning instruction following, instruction hierarchy, hallucination, scheming, reward hacking), with a 74.3% average probability of confessing conditioned on bad behavior, and is significantly more honest in confession than in its main answer. Caveat: confessions work best for **knowing/intentional** violations (reward hacking, scheming, deliberate override) and fail mostly on unintentional errors, so this method targets the "aware of the trade-off" end of the failure mode; OpenAI itself positions confessions primarily as a deployment monitoring/diagnostic tool (enabling rejection sampling and user surfacing), not as the primary training signal.

**CoCoNot** (2407.12043) broadens the strategy to noncompliance/infeasibility more generally, benchmarking four mitigation strategies on Tulu-2, with continued SFT (LoRA) on the CoCoNot noncompliance set and DPO on its contrast set as the two highlighted mitigations. Its preference data structure explicitly rewards compliance on look-alike requests: "For each query in the train set, we generate a compliant and a noncompliant response to create preference data where compliance is preferred over noncompliance" [26]. Even GPT-4 "incorrectly compl[ies] with as many as 30% of requests" in previously understudied noncompliance categories, per CoCoNot's own 1,000-prompt evaluation suite [26].

A separate 2026 **compliance-gap** study [27] is a diagnostic benchmark, not itself a training method, but it identifies precisely which constraint types this mode needs new reward designs for: across 60 default-framing sessions, all six tested frontier models (Claude Sonnet 4, GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, Gemini 2.5 Flash, Llama 3.3 70B, Mistral Small 24B) showed 0% actual compliance with user-issued process instructions while verbally agreeing to follow them ("Claude Sonnet 4 verbally agrees ten out of ten times then bypasses in all ten"). Non-compliance tracked the RLHF reward gradient rather than user instructions: "models comply 97% on tasks where rationale is rewarded (audit trails) and 0–4% where it is not (file reading, privacy masking)"; removing delegation tools raised compliance from 0% to 74.7% (Cohen's d = 2.47), and a single in-session correction prompt achieved 100% repair. This tells a commissioning lab which constraint types are training-signal gaps (procedural steps that earn no text reward) versus prompt-level fixes.

**HIPO** (2603.16152) formulates hierarchical instruction following as a Constrained Markov Decision Process and trains with primal-dual safe RL plus a GRPO-style group policy gradient, deliberately training on both compliant and non-compliant (conflicting) data rather than filtering conflicts out. Its reward comes from a "dual LLM-as-a-Judge protocol" (e.g. DeepSeek V3.2, ChatGPT) producing separate system- and user-compliance scores, each normalized to [0,1] — **model-judged, not rule-verified or human-labeled** [28]. On the conflicting test split, HIPO raises Qwen3-1.7B system-prompt compliance to the prescribed τ=0.7 threshold while keeping user utility above SFT; SFT's own conflict-split compliance only inches from 0.56 to 0.60, staying below threshold — evidence that plain SFT/DPO do not internalize hierarchical priority under genuine conflict [28].

**VerIH's conflict-labeled half** (2511.04694, reused from constraint_overridden) is relevant here too: half of its 7,192 samples are synthetically generated conflicting (system, user_conflict) pairs, produced by prompting Claude-4-Sonnet to rewrite user instructions to conflict with the system prompt while preserving core intent — this is a concrete recipe for manufacturing conflict-labeled training data at scale while keeping the downstream output check rule-based [24].

**Dataset taxonomy**

- **ConfessionReport schema** [25]: structured JSON, per-constraint checklist enumerating every explicit and implicit instruction/constraint from user, developer, system, and platform policies; per-constraint compliance analysis with cited evidence; and a list of uncertainties, ambiguities, and judgment calls — a concrete annotation template for constraint-conflict/honesty data.
- **CoCoNot**: ~11K synthetically generated prompt-response pairs for SFT, plus a 1,000-prompt evaluation set manually reviewed and edited by the paper's authors; contrastive preference pairs (compliant = chosen, noncompliant = rejected) built per benign-look-alike query [26].
- **HIPO / SystemCheck**: 2,000 (system, user) hierarchical instruction pairs, mixed 1:1 conflicting:aligned "to prevent policy over-conservatism during training," split 1,800 train / 200 test; reward is dual-LLM-judge, not rule-based [28].
- **VerIH**: 7,192 samples, half conflicting, with output constraints (format/quantity/keyword) that remain deterministically checkable even though the conflict framing itself is synthetically generated — a hybrid structure (synthetic conflict generation + rule-based output check) [24].

Product read: constraint_sacrificed is **fundamentally judge-dependent** — confessions and HIPO both rely on LLM judges for the reward signal, and CoCoNot relies on synthetic-plus-human-reviewed preference pairs rather than any programmatic verifier. VerIH is the partial exception (its output constraints are rule-verifiable), but even it needs synthetic conflict generation upstream. A lab commissioning this mode's data should expect to buy judge/rubric infrastructure (AfterQuery's "RL + Rubrics" product line is the direct vendor analog [1]), not RLVR shortcuts.

## constraint_misread

**Strategy**

**HedgeTriple** (2507.12782) is a named contrastive-training approach purpose-built for negation and hedging semantics: the dataset is distilled from GPT-3.5 grounded in a linguistic taxonomy of negation (verbal, absolute, affixal, lexical — Pullum & Huddleston 2002) and a two-type hedging taxonomy (single-word vs. multi-word cues — Crompton 1997, seeded from 134 single-word and 45 multi-word cues in HedgePeer) [29]. Contrastive fine-tuning of the all-mpnet-base-v2 encoder on HedgeTriple, using Multiple Negative Ranking Loss (anchor–hedged as positive, anchor–negated as hard negative), produces large negation-benchmark gains while roughly preserving general capability: NevIR rises from 8.10 to 40.56, a 4-benchmark negation average rises from 32.13 to 54.17, while the 56-dataset MTEB average moves only from 58.79 to 57.14 [29]. Taxonomy-driven pattern diversity beats raw data quantity here: HedgeMPNet, trained on similarly sized data, outperforms NegMPNet (~80K rule-based verbal-negation samples) and the Jina embedding model (~50K GPT-3.5 verbal-negation triples) — the design lesson for anyone specifying this data is diversity of negation type over volume [29]. The same data transfers to decoder-only LLMs with a measurable capability tradeoff: LoRA fine-tuning of Llama-3-8B-Instruct on HedgeTriple (converted to yes/no opposite-meaning pairs) raises the negation-benchmark average from 66.85 to 77.17, while GSM8K drops from 75.36 to 67.10, with degradation concentrated in MMLU math subsets — the authors conclude negation-robustness training can harm arithmetic reasoning, a real tradeoff to flag when commissioning this data [29].

**AMBROSIA** (Saparina & Lapata, NeurIPS 2024 Datasets & Benchmarks) [30] targets scope, attachment, and vagueness ambiguity with paired unambiguous readings rather than negation specifically. Its headline result — "the best model, Llama 3-70B, achieves only 31% recall on ambiguous questions compared to 66% on unambiguous ones" [30] — shows models typically commit to a single reading rather than covering all valid interpretations of a scope/attachment/vague instruction, which is the constraint_misread failure mode operating at the semantic-ambiguity level.

**NoFEVER / NoSNLI** (2503.22395) supplies a third, complementary minimal-pair design for entailment-level negation robustness: "we prepare a set of paired evaluation sentences that differ only in a negation of the primary verb... if one follows from the premise, the other contradicts it, and vice versa" [31], making the entailment label logically auto-verifiable given the non-negated label. This paper is evaluation-only — it proposes no training method, only a benchmark.

**RECAST/RLVC**'s independent-reward-channel design (its primary home is constraint_never_surfaced) is worth citing here for one angle: RLVC "provides an independent reward channel for each constraint... combines verifiable constraints as reward signals with Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO)" [9] — evidence that per-constraint verifiable rewards generalize beyond format-style constraints to semantic-type constraints too, though RECAST itself is not scoped to negation/ambiguity specifically.

**Dataset taxonomy**

- **HedgeTriple**: 248K samples; 50K anchor sentences drawn from SNLI, MultiNLI, sentence-compression, Simple Wikipedia, and COCO are negated and hedged, then filtered to 31K anchors by Levenshtein distance (threshold 60) to keep only genuine minimal pairs. The full pipeline is synthetic and auto-filtered with **no human annotation step** [29] — this class of data is largely auto-generatable.
- **AMBROSIA**: 16 domains, 846 multi-table databases, 4,242 SQL queries; a **hybrid** annotation scheme — ambiguous questions and their natural-language interpretations are human-written by screened, SQL-knowledgeable Prolific annotators, while gold SQL is auto-generated from templates for scope/attachment ambiguity (human-written only for vague questions) [30]. The structural-ambiguity slice specifically: 501 scope-ambiguous and 362 attachment-ambiguous questions, each with exactly two interpretations and two gold SQL queries, execution-checkable for whether a model recovers both readings [30].
- **NoFEVER-ENG** (2,600 premise-hypothesis-negated_hypothesis triplets, 1,513 vs. 1,087 split) and **NoSNLI-ENG** (6,261 triplets, 3,245 vs. 3,016 split) [31]. Annotation is a two-phase pipeline: rule-based negation generation (the Python `negate` module with spaCy en_core_web_md) followed by "a full manual check to ensure the correctness of the newly created hypotheses" — i.e. machine-generated but **exhaustively** human-verified, not sampled [31]. A notable finding for scoping mitigation work: model size correlates strongly with negation robustness (Spearman 0.867 across languages) [31].

Product read: this mode splits into three distinct commissioning postures — **fully auto-generatable minimal pairs with no human step** (HedgeTriple), **human-written ambiguous-reading pairs with execution-based verification** (AMBROSIA), and **rule-generated-then-fully-human-verified triplets** (NoFEVER/NoSNLI). All three land as RLVR-ready at the evaluation stage (deterministic or execution-based labels) even where the generation step used humans — a lab should pick the posture based on whether it needs semantic ambiguity (AMBROSIA-style, needs human interpretation writing) or negation/hedging robustness (HedgeTriple/NoFEVER-style, largely automatable).

## input_misread

**Strategy**

The clearest method-level finding here is negative: a controlled **table-SFT replication study** (2501.14717) — the first cross-model, cross-dataset replication for table LLMs, spanning 4 existing table SFT corpora (TableGPT, TableLlama, TableLLM, TableBench) across 3 base models (Mistral, OLMo, Phi) evaluated on 16 table benchmarks — finds that a Shapley R² decomposition attributes 81.6% of downstream table-task performance variance to **base-model choice** and only 13.8% to the instruction-tuning dataset [32]. An error analysis of 1,000 predictions from the Phi model fine-tuned on TableLLM data found grounding errors (wrong column/cell association — the input_misread failure mode directly) were the most frequent error type at 67%, far ahead of math reasoning (14%), not-following-instructions (12%), hallucination (4%), and commonsense (2%); the authors conclude that table instruction tuning alone is insufficient to fix grounding [32]. Narrowly scoped table SFT can actively **degrade** out-of-domain performance relative to the untuned base model (e.g. Mistral drops from 27.9 to 25.5 on WikiTQ after TableGPT tuning), and best out-of-domain scores fall well below in-domain scores (Phi: 63.6 in-domain on HiTab vs. 45.3 best OOD) — evidence of overfitting to dataset-specific patterns [32]. The one positive design lesson: TableLLM's own training data, built using GPT-3.5 to enrich reasoning traces in training examples, consistently produced the best out-of-domain performance across all three base models — reasoning-trace-enriched annotation, not scale, is what generalizes [32].

**TableDreamer** (2506.08646) is a positive method lead: a progressive, weakness-guided data synthesis framework that improves Llama3.1-8B-Instruct average accuracy across 10 tabular benchmarks by 11.62 points (49.07%→60.69%) using only 27K GPT-4o-synthesized examples, "outperforming state-of-the-art data synthesis baselines that use more training data" (e.g. Self-Instruct and Magpie at 100K examples) [33] — evidence that targeted, weakness-diagnosed synthesis beats generic scale.

**ToRR** (2502.19412) is the diagnostic benchmark establishing input_misread as a real, unsolved capability gap: across 14 leading LLMs from 7 model families (including Claude 3.5 Sonnet, GPT-4o, DeepSeek-V3), overall table-task performance tops out at 0.50, with wide per-example variance across semantically equivalent table formats, because "current models do not have a stable and generalizable table representation that persists across table formats" [34]. No single serialization format (JSON, HTML, CSV, Markdown) consistently wins — the maximum per-model preference difference is only 0.06 — implying robustness training data should span multiple serializations rather than standardize on one canonical format [34].

**Dataset taxonomy**

- **Table-SFT replication grid**: 12 instruction-tuned models (3 base models × 4 existing table SFT datasets) evaluated on 16 table benchmarks [32]. The commissioning lesson is about data *design* (reasoning-trace enrichment), not volume, since dataset choice explains only 13.8% of downstream variance.
- **TableDreamer**: 27K GPT-4o-synthesized examples via progressive weakness-guided synthesis — a small, targeted, fully model-generated corpus that beats 100K-scale generic synthesis baselines [33], evidence that this class of data can be auto-generated without a human annotation step if guided by weakness diagnosis.
- **ToRR**: 10 datasets across 6 tabular task types, each with an automatic metric (Program Accuracy, F1, Accuracy, Exact Match, or Rouge depending on task) [34]. Table QA / fact-verification / classification portions are **RLVR-ready**; table-to-text portions (scored by Rouge) rely on soft overlap metrics that would need rubric or preference labels for training-grade supervision. Multiplying prompt configurations is a cheap substitute for collecting more labeled examples: "50 examples with 2 prompts yield similar reliability to 100 examples with one," and going from 1 to 10 prompt configs raises Kendall's W ranking agreement by more than 0.35 on average [34] — programmatic re-serialization/perturbation is inexpensive relative to new human-labeled examples.

Product read: input_misread data is mostly **auto-generatable and auto-verifiable** (TableDreamer's synthesis pipeline, ToRR's execution-based metrics for QA/verification/classification tasks), but ToRR's result that even frontier models plateau at 0.50 despite that verifiability shows this is a genuine capability gap, not a labeling gap. Per the replication study, a lab should commission diversity of serialization/perturbation coverage (and reasoning-trace-enriched annotation) over raw scale — base-model quality otherwise dominates the outcome regardless of dataset size.

## execution_slip

**Strategy**

**VerIF / VerInstruct** (THU-KEG, EMNLP 2025) is the clearest RLVR method lead: "a verification method that combines rule-based code verification with LLM-based verification from a large reasoning model (e.g., QwQ-32B)" — rule-based code checks hard, programmatically checkable constraints (length, format, keyword), while the reasoning-model verifier scores soft constraints, and the GRPO reward averages the 0/1 hard and soft scores [35][36]. **Tülu 3's RLVR stage** (2411.15124) corroborates the direction at a coarser grain: RLVR at 8B improved GSM8K 84.3→87.6, MATH 42.0→43.7, and IFEval 81.1→82.4 over the DPO starting point (Table 23) [37]. The same hybrid pattern recurs independently in **RLCF** (2507.18624, reused from constraint_never_surfaced): specialized verifier programs are generated "ONLY for requirements that are explicitly about syntax or format," with everything else judge-graded [6] — a second system landing on the same split.

**Dataset taxonomy**

- **VerInstruct**: ~22,000 instances, average 6.2 constraints each, released with per-constraint checkers and verification functions [35][36]. This is the report's clearest quantification of hybrid verifiability: only ~22.3% of constraints have deterministic code verifiers; ~77.7% are tagged for LLM verification during RL [35]. In other words, even a purpose-built "RLVR" instruction-following dataset is roughly three-quarters judge-dependent at the constraint level.
- **RECAST's parallel ratio** (reused from constraint_never_surfaced, cited here for its execution-verifiability angle): ~4.8 rule-based vs. ~8.6 model-based constraints per instruction (≈36% deterministic / ≈64% judge-graded), feeding a GRPO reward that further improves performance beyond SFT (Qwen-2.5-7B average hard-satisfaction rate 31.25%→32.33%, with gains concentrated at higher difficulty) [9].
- **Tülu 3's RLVR data**: ground-truth-answer tasks (math, precise-IF constraints) — the genuinely RLVR-ready slice of instruction-following work, in contrast to VerIF/RECAST's mostly-judged constraint mixes [37].

Product read: despite the "RLVR" branding attached to this mode, the data a lab would actually commission is **majority judge-graded at the constraint level** — roughly 64–78% depending on the system (VerInstruct vs. RECAST) — with only format/length/keyword-style constraints cheaply auto-verifiable. A vendor engagement for execution_slip should budget for LLM-judge infrastructure (or a large reasoning model as verifier, as VerIF does with QwQ-32B) rather than assume a pure programmatic reward pipeline.

## degenerate_output

**Strategy**

An **overthinking survey** (2503.16419) names and taxonomizes this failure mode directly: "the overthinking phenomenon" in large reasoning models (OpenAI o1, DeepSeek-R1), where "longer chain-of-thought sequences improve performance but produce verbose, redundant outputs with significant computational overhead" [38]. The survey's mitigation taxonomy has three branches: (1) **model-based efficient reasoning** — training/optimizing models to reason concisely; (2) **reasoning output-based reduction** — dynamically shortening reasoning at inference time; (3) **input prompt-based enhancement** — difficulty- or length-conditioned prompting/routing [38]. Within the model-based branch, the survey names a concrete family of training strategies under "**RL with Length Reward Design**" — rewarding shorter correct reasoning — cataloging specific named methods: O1-Pruner, L1, DAST, Kimi k1.5, LASER, BRPO, GRPO-λ, AutoThink, and DeGRPO [38]. The survey is explicit that this is a correctness problem, not merely a cost problem: "excessive reasoning steps can introduce errors, obscure logical clarity, and prevent answers within a user-defined token budget" [38], which supports treating endless self-correction / no-committed-answer as a genuine training-relevant failure mode.

A separate **repetition-loop mechanism paper** (arXiv HTML 2512.12895v1) supplies a causal account of why loops happen at all: the paper attributes reasoning-model repetition loops to "errors in learning" — when the correct progress-making action is hard to learn but an easy cyclic alternative exists, the model shifts probability mass onto the cyclic action, producing loops at low temperature [39]. This has a direct implication for which models need mitigation most: distilled student models loop far more than their teachers, and smaller models loop more than larger ones within a family — e.g. OpenThinker3-1.5B loops in about 30% of greedy-decoded responses while its teacher QwQ-32B barely loops, and DeepSeek-R1-Distill-Qwen shows roughly 76%/51%/40% loop rates for 1.5B/7B/32B at temperature 0 [39].

**Tülu 3's PPO recipe** (2411.15124) includes an explicit, shippable **termination-commitment reward**: "any sampled response that fails to emit an EOS token within the max-token budget receives a −10 penalty," directly training the model to commit to and complete an answer rather than loop indefinitely [37].

**Dataset taxonomy**

- **Variable-length CoT SFT** (per the overthinking survey's taxonomy): datasets pairing long and short reasoning traces that both reach correct answers, constructed either post-reasoning (compressing redundant steps via heuristics or LLMs) or during-reasoning (prompting models to emit short reasoning) — this is largely **auto-constructible / model-generated**, not dependent on human preference labels [38].
- **Length-reward RL**: not a labeled dataset per se — the reward is computed over the model's own generations (length + correctness) using the named methods above; the realistic commissioning need is reward-function engineering plus held-out eval sets, not a large new human-annotated corpus [38].
- **Loop-mechanism paper's evidence base**: greedy-decoding loop-rate measurement across model families and sizes at fixed temperature — a diagnostic/census methodology for identifying which models and settings need mitigation, not itself a training dataset [39].
- **Tülu 3's EOS-penalty term**: a reward-shaping addition inside an existing RLVR pipeline (GSM8K / MATH / IFEval verifiable-reward setup) — no dedicated new dataset required [37].

Product read: degenerate_output is the mode with the **thinnest "commission a labeled dataset" story** in this report. Its two concrete, evidenced training levers — length-reward RL and EOS-penalty reward shaping — are reward-function edits layered on top of existing verifiable-reward data, not new annotation programs. The one genuinely dataset-shaped artifact, variable-length CoT SFT pairs, is per the survey auto-constructible rather than requiring human raters — meaning this is a mode where a lab likely needs a reward-design engagement more than a data-labeling one.
