Where small open models fail on hard instructions, and what actually fixes it

This project runs constraint-dense prompts through open models, grades every criterion with a blind multi-family judge panel, diagnoses each failure's root cause i, then tests whether the cause should pick the training method.

Surge AI ComplexConstraints (75 prompts) · Qwen3.5 9B/35B/397B + Llama-3.2-3B · 4-family judge panel · pre-registered training experiment
Key findings
65%
Two causes dominate failure: constraints the model never engages (coverage i, 28.8%) and constraints it engages but botches (precision i, 36.4%), out of 882 diagnosed failures in the Llama-3.2-3B census.
Labels are classifier-assigned (95% single-classifier); the census covers one model on one benchmark.
See the census →
−0.34
The pre-registered hypothesis reversed. GRPO i was predicted to beat SFT most on precision; instead its edge was 4× larger on coverage (interaction i −0.34, CI [−0.41, −0.27]). GRPO recovered 74% of coverage failures; SFT recovered 28% and broke 55% of what already worked.
A truncation confound was investigated and ruled out; the reversal survives every sensitivity variant.
Read the result →
74% → 7.6%
Only the cautionary finding transferred. On naturalistic prompts GRPO's coverage win collapsed (74% to 7.6% recovery) while SFT's destructiveness replicated on every distribution tested: on-task breakage, a ≈3-point MMLU regression, and naturalistic breakage.
Transfer eval is descriptive and single-judge; a ≈20% false-recovery floor bounds its absolute numbers.
